About

I came to finance from physics — and I still read a portfolio the way I read an experiment: find the real distribution, question every assumption, and never trust a number you can't defend.

That instinct is what model risk is actually about. At Accenture and Deloitte I've built and independently validated the models behind credit decisions worth billions — scorecards, PD term structures, loss forecasts, stress tests — and then sat in the room when they were challenged.

I care about models that are transparent, challenger-tested, and built to survive the cycle — not engineered to clear a review and fail when the macro turns. I'm as comfortable defending a forecast to a Model Risk committee as I am writing the SAS or Python behind it.

Open source · Model risk tooling

Model Inventory & Risk Tracker

A working model-risk register that tiers every model, schedules its revalidation, and tracks findings through to closure.

Most model inventories live in a spreadsheet. This is the one I wanted instead. Every model is scored on five factors — materiality, complexity, reliance, regulatory impact and uncertainty — into a weighted Tier 1/2/3 rating, and that tier drives the revalidation clock, flagging each model overdue, due soon or current. Findings are logged against models and tracked to resolution, with a dashboard over the top. It ships with a written methodology, CSV import/export for the Excel workflow teams actually use, and a Docker path for internal hosting — seeded with a fictional bank inventory so it's usable the moment it opens.

Perspective

Model risk isn't a compliance tax — done right, it's how an institution earns the confidence to lend. A few things I like arguing about:

  • What IFRS 9 staging really does once the macro turns — and why the cliff-edge rarely sits where the model puts it.
  • Challenger models aren't a beauty contest. SR 11-7 is really asking whether your production model can lose an argument.
  • Explainability that survives a validation — SHAP built for the committee isn't the same as SHAP that actually explains the score.
  • Where GenAI genuinely helps model risk — documentation, anomaly flagging, challenger scaffolding — and where it quietly doesn't.

If any of these land differently for you, I'd genuinely like to hear it.
